# Figure Skating Learning Plan

**Profile:** 32-year-old male | WIFA figure skates | Some ice experience
**Goal:** Structured progression from beginner fundamentals to advanced skills
**Estimated timeline:** 18-24 months (skating 2-3x/week + off-ice training)

---

## Your Skates: WIFA Notes

WIFA (Vienna, est. 1920) makes stiff, supportive leather boots — built for ice originally. This is *good news* for you as an adult learner:

- **High ankle support** — protective during falls and landing jumps
- **Stiff boot** — you'll need a proper break-in period (15-20 sessions of gradual use). Don't crank them fully tight at first; leave the top hook loose for ankle flex until the leather softens.
- **Flex notch** (if you have Champion Light or similar) — helps with knee bend, critical for edges and jumps
- Make sure your blades are **freshly sharpened** with a standard 7/16" hollow (ask for this at any pro shop). Dull blades are the #1 hidden obstacle for adult beginners.

---

## How This Plan Works

The plan follows the **Learn to Skate USA Adult Curriculum (Levels 1-6)** as its structural backbone, then extends into freestyle (jumps/spins) and ice dance. Each phase has:

1. **On-ice skills** — what to practice each session
2. **YouTube tutorials** — specific videos to watch *before* your session
3. **Off-ice training** — strength/flexibility work for days you're not at the rink
4. **Milestone** — how you know you're ready to move on

**Key principle for adult males:** You likely have more raw strength than flexibility. Your #1 limiting factor will be ankle/hip flexibility and the courage to commit to edges, not power. Every phase includes targeted mobility work.

- Wall sits: 3 x 45 sec (mimics skating posture)
- Calf raises: 3 x 15 (ankle strength for edge control)
- Hip circles: 10 each direction (hip mobility)
- Lunges: 3 x 10 each leg

## Adult Male-Specific Tips

1. **Knee bend is everything.** Men tend to skate too upright. Exaggerate your knee bend until it feels ridiculous — that's probably correct.

2. **Arms matter.** Check your arms constantly. T-rex arms or flailing arms kill your balance. Watch the Coach Mary videos — she's excellent on upper body.

3. **Your weight is an asset for jumps.** Adult men generate more power on takeoffs than most learn-to-skate peers. The challenge is *controlling* the landing, not getting airborne.

4. **Flexibility is your bottleneck.** Spend 2x more time on hip flexors and ankles than you think you need. A 32-year-old male hip is not a 12-year-old girl's hip, and that's fine — adjust expectations on spirals and laybacks, and focus on power moves, spins, and jump quality.

5. **WIFA boot break-in**: Your stiff WIFA boots will resist deep knee bends at first. This is temporary. Skate 20-minute sessions the first week, building to full sessions over 3 weeks. Don't heat-mold them yourself — take them to a pro shop if needed.

6. **Get a coach.** YouTube is excellent supplemental material, but nothing replaces a coach watching you in real-time. Most rinks offer adult group lessons for around $15-20/session, or private lessons for $30-50/half hour. A coach every 2 weeks + YouTube self-study is optimal for budget and progress.

7. **Join the community.** r/FigureSkating on Reddit has a large adult skater community. The "Adults Skate Too" brand (https://adultsskatetoo.com) runs community events and has excellent blog guides.

8. **Protective gear is not embarrassing.** Padded shorts (crash pads), wrist guards, and even a helmet for jump practice are smart, not soft. Your 32-year-old body takes longer to heal than a teenager's.
